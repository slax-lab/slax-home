Over the past month or so, the biggest change in Slax Reader is a rebuilt reading page — the page you see when you open a saved article. One change touched 182 files, followed by three weeks of polish. Around this redesign, AI chat, highlights, comments, and sharing each got an upgrade too.

## The reading page: your cached articles, presented properly

Every article you save gets a full cached copy in Slax Reader. If the original page gets deleted, edited, or taken down, your copy is still there. The point of this redesign is to make that copy comfortable to read:

- Open an article and the center holds nothing but the text. Table of contents, AI overview, AI chat, and comments all live in a side panel — open what you need, and nothing shows up uninvited. On mobile, these panels become a bottom sheet, right where your thumb is
- Tables got a new layout: zebra striping, retuned line spacing and padding, and wide tables no longer collapse into a mess
- Page transitions now animate, so moving around no longer feels abrupt

The bookmark list changed in three ways: click anywhere on a card to open the article, no need to aim for the title; switching between card view and text view morphs in place instead of flashing the whole page; and long lists now use virtual scrolling, so thousands of bookmarks scroll without lag.

## AI chat: point at an image and ask

Asking the AI used to mean asking about the whole article. Now you can select a passage and ask about just that — or quote an image from the article.

This is most useful with technical articles: architecture diagrams, data charts, anything words don't quite explain. Hand the image to the AI and let it tell you what's in it.

## Highlights and comments, connected

Highlights used to be highlights and comments used to be comments. Now they're two steps of the same thing:

- Any highlight can take a comment later — just tap it and write
- Click any entry in the comments panel and the article scrolls to the exact spot
- Clicking an existing highlight opens a menu: add a comment, or remove the highlight

So when you highlight, you don't have to stop and think "should I write something?" Highlight first; the words can come later.

## Sharing: you decide how much to show

Every page you share now has three switches:

- Whether to show your name and avatar
- Whether to show your highlights and comments
- Whether visitors can add their own highlights and comments

Share links changed too: they now use random UUIDs, so links can't be guessed and reveal nothing about your library. And when you share to X, the post text is filled in for you.
