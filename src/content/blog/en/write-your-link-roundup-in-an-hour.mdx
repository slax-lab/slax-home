If you write a weekly link roundup, you already know where the time goes. It isn't the writing. It's the rebuilding.

By Sunday, you have forty links scattered across browser tabs, bookmarks, a notes app and a chat with yourself. You remember that one piece was brilliant, but not which paragraph made you think so. So you open everything again, reread half of it, and try to recover the thought you had on Tuesday.

The fix is to do the thinking once, at the moment you read. Then writing day becomes assembly.

> **Read once, keep the line you'd quote, write down why. On writing day, your issue is already half written.**

This guide shows how to set that up with Slax Reader. It takes five minutes, and it works whether you publish on Substack, Beehiiv, Ghost, Kit or anywhere else.

## What changes for you

- **Your take survives the week.** You write one sentence on why a piece matters while it's fresh, not four days later.
- **Every quote is in one place.** All your highlights from all your saves appear together, ready to copy.
- **Your sources don't break.** Slax Reader keeps a lasting copy of every page you save, along with the original link. If a site edits or deletes the article, you still have what you read.
- **You stop rereading.** For a long piece you only skimmed, Slax Reader Pro can outline it or answer a question about it, so you can decide quickly whether it makes the cut.
- **It goes where you read.** Save from Chrome, from your phone's share button, or by sending a link to the Slax Telegram bot. Everything syncs between the web and your phone.

## Set it up once

1. **Create your account** at [r.slax.com](https://r.slax.com/) and install the [Chrome extension](/blog/quick-save-with-the-slax-extension/) and the phone app.
2. **Make one tag for your newsletter**, such as `roundup`. If you like to plan by issue, make a tag per issue instead, such as `issue-142`.
3. **Pick your "it made the cut" signal.** We suggest the star. Save freely, and star only the pieces that will go in the issue.

Already have years of saves in Pocket, Omnivore, Instapaper or Readwise? You can [bring them over](/blog/import-pocket-bookmarks/) from Settings → Data.

## During the week: read with the issue in mind

Do the same three things every time something is worth sharing.

**Highlight the one line you'd quote.** Select the text and highlight it. Keep it to one or two sentences. This is the line your readers will see.

**Add a comment with your take.** Write one sentence, the way you'd write it in the issue: "The best explanation of why ad prices fell this year." That comment is your blurb, already drafted.

**Tag it and star it.** Tag it `roundup`, and star it if it's definitely in. Everything else can wait in your inbox until writing day.

If you're on your phone, it's the same: share the article to Slax Reader, open it later, highlight and comment. Highlights and comments sync between the web and your phone.

## On writing day: assemble, don't rebuild

Open Slax Reader on the web and work through three views.

- **Starred.** This is your shortlist. Most weeks, it's already the right length.
- **Highlights.** Every line you marked, across every article, in one list. Copy each quote straight into your editor.
- **Your tag.** Open `roundup` or `issue-142` to see anything you saved but didn't star. It's the place to look for a last-minute addition.

For each item, paste the quote, paste your comment as the blurb, then tidy the wording. Link to the original source, which Slax Reader keeps on every save.

Short on time for a long piece you saved but never finished? Open it and use the AI outline (Pro) to see its main points in a minute, or ask it a question such as "What's the one claim here that people will argue with?" Then decide whether it belongs in this issue.

## A real week, start to finish

**Monday.** You read a long essay on the future of local news over coffee. You highlight one sentence, comment "Read this if you only read one thing this week", tag `roundup` and star it.

**Wednesday.** A friend sends a link in a group chat. You tap share, then Slax Reader, and keep scrolling. You'll read it on Thursday's commute.

**Thursday.** On the train, you read it on your phone. It's good but not great. You tag it `roundup` and don't star it.

**Saturday.** You save six more pieces from Chrome while browsing. Two get stars and highlights.

**Sunday.** You open Starred: five pieces. You open Highlights: five quotes and five comments. You paste them into your editor, write an intro, and add Thursday's article as a "one more thing". The issue is done in under an hour.
